# Business-Intelligence-Analysis-for-Retail-Food-Pricing-and-Sales-in-Malaysia
## Overview
The food retail industry in Malaysia is continuously evolving due to changing consumer preferences, fluctuating food prices, and increasing market competition. These changes highlight the importance of data-driven decision-making in understanding retail market conditions, monitoring pricing trends, evaluating sales performance, and identifying opportunities for business improvement. 
This project applies Business Intelligence (BI) and data analytics to explore Malaysia's food retail industry by integrating food price information and historical retail sales data. The primary objective is to transform raw datasets into meaningful insights through interactive dashboards, enabling stakeholders to better understand food pricing patterns, product distribution, retail premises, and sales performance across different categories and geographical regions.
## Dataset
This project utilises two primary datasets to analyse Malaysia’s food retail industry. The first dataset, Malaysia’s Food Price Data in Premise (PriceCatcher: 2024/05), contains food price information across various retail premises, states, and districts in Malaysia. It consists of three interconnected tables: the lookup item dataset, which provides item codes, names, groups, and categories; the lookup premise dataset, which contains premise codes, names, types, states, and districts; and the PriceCatcher dataset, which records food prices based on item codes and premise codes. 
These datasets are linked through their respective codes to enable comprehensive food price and distribution analysis. The second dataset, Malaysia’s Retail Food Sales Data (Alexandre Holm Perrault, 2024), contains historical and forecasted sales data across different food categories. It is used to identify sales patterns, evaluate category performance, and examine changes in retail sales over time. By integrating these two datasets and cross-referencing their food categories, the project provides a broader understanding of food pricing, retail distribution, and sales performance in Malaysia.
## Analysis
The project is divided into four main analytical sections: 
- Overview Analysis: Provides a general summary of food item and retail premise distribution, including monthly comparisons and average item prices. 
- Item Analysis: Examines food item distribution, category performance, average pricing, and monthly price trends, including price forecasting. 
- Premise Analysis: Explores the distribution of retail premises across different states and districts, including premise categories and geographical pricing differences. 
- Sales Analysis: Evaluates historical retail sales performance across food categories, compares yearly sales, and identifies growth trends using metrics such as Growth Rate and Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR).
### Overview
The Overview Dashboard provides a general summary of Malaysia's food retail landscape by presenting the distribution of food items and retail premises across different categories and months. The Total Items by Month chart shows an increase in the number of items from 365 in May to 392 in June, indicating a growth in recorded item availability. Meanwhile, the Total Premises by Month chart shows relatively stable retail coverage, with a slight decrease from 65 premises in May to 64 in June. The dashboard also highlights the distribution of items by group, where Ready To Cook Food represents the largest group with 191 items, and the distribution of premises by category, with supermarkets being the predominant premise type. Additionally, the Top 10 Average Item Price chart identifies items with the highest average prices, while the detailed table provides a breakdown of item prices across different states. These visualisations offer stakeholders an overall understanding of item availability, retail distribution, and pricing variations to support business monitoring and decision-making.

### Premise Analysis
The Premise Analysis Dashboard examines the geographical distribution and characteristics of retail premises across different states and districts in Malaysia. The Total Premises by Month chart indicates relatively stable premise availability, with a slight decrease from 65 locations in May to 64 in June. The Total Premises by Premise Category pie chart illustrates the distribution of retail formats, showing that supermarkets represent the largest category of premises. Meanwhile, the Total Premises by State and Total Premises by District charts identify areas with the highest retail presence, with Johor and W.P. Kuala Lumpur recording the highest numbers of premises among the states, while Johor Bahru leads in district-level distribution. The Total Items by Premise Name chart provides insights into item availability across individual retail locations, allowing comparisons between premises. Additionally, the detailed table presents premise information by state and district alongside average item prices, supporting further geographical analysis. These visualisations help stakeholders understand retail market coverage, identify areas with concentrated business activity, and evaluate potential opportunities for market expansion and resource allocation.

### Sales Analysis
The Sales Analysis Dashboard evaluates historical retail sales performance across different food categories by comparing sales figures between 2023 and 2024. The Total Sales in Million by Item Category chart highlights the categories contributing the most to overall revenue, with Staple Foods and Rice, Pasta and Noodles emerging as the leading categories. The Total Items by Item Category chart provides additional context by illustrating the distribution of products across different food categories, including Side Dish and Drinks. Furthermore, a detailed sales table presents historical sales figures, annual growth rates, and Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) for each item category, allowing users to identify categories with positive or declining sales performance over time. By combining sales comparisons with long-term growth indicators, the dashboard provides a clearer understanding of retail sales trends, category performance, and potential opportunities for business planning, marketing strategies, and inventory management.

## Insight Discussion
The analysis provides several important insights into Malaysia's food retail industry, particularly regarding item availability, pricing trends, retail distribution, and sales performance. The increase in the total number of recorded food items from May to June, alongside the relatively stable number of retail premises, indicates a positive trend in product availability. The distribution of items across different groups and categories, particularly Ready To Cook Food and Side Dish, provides an understanding of product variety and category representation in the retail market. Meanwhile, the price prediction analysis highlights potential price fluctuations, while the highest average-priced items offer insights into pricing differences among food products. 
From a geographical perspective, Johor and W.P. Kuala Lumpur demonstrate a strong retail presence, with Johor Bahru recording the highest number of premises among the districts. This information helps identify areas with significant retail activity and potential opportunities for market expansion. Furthermore, the sales analysis identifies Staple Foods and Rice, Pasta and Noodles as the leading revenue-generating categories. The inclusion of annual growth rates and Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) also provides a better understanding of long-term sales performance and changing market trends. Overall, combining food price data with historical retail sales information provides a comprehensive view of Malaysia's food retail landscape and valuable insights to support data-driven decisions in pricing strategies, inventory planning, market evaluation, and business development.
## Summary
This project demonstrates the application of Business Intelligence (BI) in analysing Malaysia's food retail industry through the integration of food price and historical retail sales datasets. By developing interactive dashboards, the analysis provides insights into food item distribution, pricing trends, retail premise characteristics, and sales performance across different categories and geographical regions. The findings highlight an increase in recorded item availability, relatively stable retail premise distribution, geographical market concentrations in Johor and W.P. Kuala Lumpur, and strong sales contributions from categories such as Staple Foods and Rice, Pasta and Noodles. Furthermore, price forecasting and historical sales analysis provide additional perspectives on potential market changes and long-term sales performance. Overall, this project demonstrates how data visualisation and analytics can transform raw data into meaningful business insights, helping stakeholders make informed decisions regarding pricing strategies, inventory planning, marketing initiatives, and retail business development.
## Recommendation
The following recommendations are proposed to improve the effectiveness and future development of the BI solutions:
- Expand Data Coverage: Incorporate additional datasets covering more months and years to provide a broader understanding of food pricing trends and retail sales performance.
- Improve Price Forecasting: Explore more advanced forecasting techniques and evaluate their accuracy to provide more reliable price predictions for future business planning.
- Enhance Inventory Management: Integrate inventory and stock availability data to identify potential stock shortages, overstocking, and high-demand products, allowing stakeholders to optimise inventory levels.
- Develop Customer Segmentation: Incorporate customer demographic and purchasing behaviour data to identify different customer groups and support more targeted marketing strategies.
- Implement Real-Time Data Integration: Establish regular or automated data updates to ensure that dashboards reflect the latest market conditions and provide more relevant insights for decision-making.
## Limitations
Despite providing valuable insights into Malaysia's food retail industry, this project has several limitations that should be considered:
- Limited Data Period: The PriceCatcher dataset used in this project covers a limited period (May 2024), which restricts the ability to analyse long-term food price movements and seasonal patterns.
- Data Availability: The analysis depends on the availability and completeness of the provided datasets. Missing records or inconsistencies may affect the accuracy and reliability of the findings.
- Dataset Integration: The food price and retail sales datasets originate from different sources and use different data structures. Therefore, matching food categories may not always provide an exact representation of the relationship between item prices and sales performance.
- Forecasting Accuracy: Price forecasting is influenced by historical data patterns and may not fully account for external factors such as inflation, economic conditions, supply disruptions, and changes in consumer demand.
- Limited Business Variables: The datasets do not include detailed information about inventory levels, customer demographics, purchasing behaviour, or operational costs, limiting the scope of deeper business performance analysis.
